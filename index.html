PC cases

PC cases protect components from physical and electrical damage and cool them down. PC cases are made primrily of steel, aluminum, plastic, metal, and sometimes glass. There are four types of cases (which are called form factors): horizontal case, full-size tower, compact tower, and all-in-one. Choose horizontal case if you have low vertical space. Choose full-size tower if you want to maximize performance, alow more expansion capabilities, or get better components, such as a bigger motherboard. Choose compact tower if you want to minimize the space that a PC takes. Or choose an all-in-one if you want the moniter to be combined with internal components. All-in-one also has minimum cable management and touch screen input, saves desk space, and reduces cable clutter.

cooling systems

Cooling systems remove heat from components. They prevent components from overheating, crashing, and weakening processor power. There are many cooling systems in a computer.

Case fans take air from outside and blow it into the computer, and blow out hot air from the computer, keeping it cool. They use elecricity and mechanical parts to force airflow over components.

Heat sinks disperse excessive heat from components. They are made of several metal fins in a block shape. The heat sink sit son top of the CPU, absorbs heat from other components into the metal fins; and then, as cooler air passes over it, the air absorbs some heat from the metal fins, and then, moves that heat away from the metal fins.

power supply units

power supply units take raw Ac electricity and transform it into safe direct current for components. the form factors of power supply units include advanced technology extended volt 12 and entry-level power supply 12 volt. advanced technology extended volt 12 are made for desktops and are the most common power supply today. entry-level power supply 12 volt is a heavy duty power supply built for computers that never turn off.
