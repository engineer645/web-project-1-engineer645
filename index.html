PC cases, cooling systems, and power supply units

PC cases

PC cases protect components from physical and electrical damage and cool them down. PC cases are made primrily of steel, aluminum, plastic, metal, and sometimes glass. There are four types of cases (which are called form factors): horizontal case, full-size tower, compact tower, and all-in-one. Choose horizontal case if you have low vertical space. Choose full-size tower if you want to maximize performance, alow more expansion capabilities, or get better components, such as a bigger motherboard. Choose compact tower if you want to minimize the space that a PC takes. Or choose an all-in-one if you want the moniter to be combined with internal components. All-in-one also has minimum cable management and touch screen input, saves desk space, and reduces cable clutter.

cooling systems

Cooling systems remove heat from components. They prevent components from overheating, crashing, and weakening processor power. There are many cooling systems in a computer. There are many types of cooling systems.

case fans

Case fans take air from outside and blow it into the computer, and blow out hot air from the computer, keeping it cool. They use elecricity and mechanical parts to force airflow over components.

heat sinks

Heat sinks disperse excessive heat from components. They are made of several metal fins in a block shape. The heat sink sit son top of the CPU, absorbs heat from other components into the metal fins; and then, as cooler air passes over it, the air absorbs some heat from the metal fins, and then moves that heat away from the components.

liquid cooling system

Liquid cooling systems use a closed loop of circulating liquid. The parts in a liquid cooling system are the pump, radiator, CPU block, fans, reservoir, tubing, fittings, and coolant. The way liquid cooling systems work depend on whether you have a custom liquid cooling system or an AIO (all-in-one) liquid cooling system. If you have a custom liquid cooling system, the pump pushes liquid from the reservoir through the tubes into the CPU block. The liquid sits on the CPU block and absorbs heat generated by the CPU. The pump continues to push more liquid through the system, which pushes warm liquid out of the CPU block and replaces it with cooler air. The warm air gets pushed through tubes to the radiator. The radiator has fans attached to it that pulls cold air toward the radiator and allows colder air to absorb heat from the liquid going through the radiator’s spiraled pipes. Once the liquid travels through the spiraled pipes, it gets pushed back into the reservoir by the pump, which completes the loop. The only difference with an AIO is that there is no reservoir, and the pump sits inside the CPU block. So, the liquid inside the CPU block absorbs heat from the CPU, then gets pushed out of the CPU block by the pump and into the radiator using tubing. Next, the liquid moves through the radiator. The fans pull cold air towards the radiator’s spiraled pipes to cool down the heated liquid. Then, the cooled liquid gets pushed back into the CPU block to complete the loop.

graphics card cooling system

Graphics cards need their own cooling system beacuse they consume the most power and generate intense heat across the PC. This makes the PC prone to throttling, slowing down performance.

power supply units (PSUs)

Power supply units are like the computer's heart and electrical filter. Turning dangerous power into safe energy for every component. They take raw Ac electricity and transform it into safe direct current for components. The form factors of power supply units include advanced technology extended volt 12 and entry-level power supply 12 volt. Advanced technology extended volt 12 are made for desktops and are the most common power supply today. Entry-level power supply 12 volt is a heavy duty power supply built for computers that never turn off. PSUs deliver multiple reguulated DC (direct current) voltages. Usually +12 V, +5 V, 3.3 V, and (-12) V, with auxiliary signaling lines for control. CPUs and GPUs use +12 V as their primary power source. USB ports, arduino boards, raspberry pi, RGB lights, and many electronic sensors rely on 5 V power. RAM, M.2, NVMe SSDs, and mothwerboard chips use 3.3 V power.

rails

Rails are like a highway dedicated to a carrying power to different cmponents. There are three types of rails: single rails, dual rails, and multi rails. In single rails, all power comes from one pool. It is simple to set up buyt has less safety protection. In dual rail, power is split into two pools — one for the CPU and the other for the GPU. In multi rails, power is split into at least three or more smaller pools. It offers the best safety protection but requires balancing high-power parts across different cables.
